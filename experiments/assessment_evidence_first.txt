Establish the governing contract before judging the reviewer's conclusion. Treat the finding as a claim to check, not as the specification of what the implementation must support.

For each finding, first identify the required behavior, adopted rule, or demonstrated design cost from the objective and repository evidence. Then check the claimed mechanism against the actual adapter and supported use. Explain the link between that evidence and the defect decision in the existing rationale field. A reproduced result is useful but not mandatory when inspection establishes a feasible failure or an explicit unmet deliverable.

Reconcile broad wording with explicit scope limits and supported operating conditions. Absence of an explicit exclusion does not by itself establish a new supported variant or stronger guarantee. An existing internal capability does not by itself promise that capability at every caller interface. When a finding asks for another rejection rule, establish why the accepted case violates the governing invariant, rather than assuming its unusual shape makes it invalid. Check the consequence at the owning interface, including any existing protection, instead of equating an intermediate difference with failure of the overall requirement.

Independently consider the strongest evidence against the claim before deciding. If the claim depends on an unestablished requirement or operating assumption, identify that assumption rather than silently adopting it. Do not use this check to erase explicit requirements, applicable standards, concrete maintenance costs, or feasible failures merely because they are minor, unfamiliar, or not covered by current tests. Keep validity separate from ownership, preserve every observation, and follow the existing output schema. Do not prescribe a repair or introduce a rejection quota.
